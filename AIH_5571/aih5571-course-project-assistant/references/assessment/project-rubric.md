# Course project rubric

Source: `AIH5571 Assessment rubrics_Project.pdf`.

This rubric is available, but the separate official Project Specification has not yet been supplied. The rubric does not authorize assumptions about topic, group size, deadlines, slide count, presentation duration, or required platform.

## Project structure

| Deliverable | Share of project | Share of subject | ILOs |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Project Proposal | 10% | 6% | a, b |
| Final Project & Technical Report | 70% | 42% | a, b, c |
| Oral Presentation & Demonstration | 20% | 12% | a, b, c |

## Proposal rubric

- Project objective formulation, background, and GenAI methodology: 60%.
- Clarity and presentation of proposal report: 30%.
- Planning of future work and labor allocation: 10%.

Top-band work requires rigorous, prioritized objectives and use cases; relevant background and constraints; justified model, technical, and prompt-engineering choices; literature support and practical feasibility; clear professional organization and diagrams; and a realistic milestone plan with risks, mitigation, deliverables, and balanced team allocation.

## Final project and technical report rubric

- Results obtained and implementation quality: 60%.
- Clarity, technical documentation, and ethical impact analysis: 30%.
- Use of GenAI engineering and system-design techniques: 10%.

Top-band work is a fully functional, robust GenAI application or workflow. The rubric values systematic prompt design, model integration and potentially fine-tuning or RAG where appropriate, qualitative and quantitative tests, error handling, validation, clean code/configuration, scholarly technical explanation, and nuanced ethical/legal/social analysis including bias, privacy, hallucination mitigation, safety, and intellectual property. Advanced engineering examples include modular architecture, robust orchestration, structured-output parsing, versioning, and context caching.

Do not force advanced techniques merely to mention them: choices must fit the released specification and project problem.

## Oral presentation and demonstration rubric

- Project and system demonstration: 50%.
- Delivery, visual slides, and Q&A: 50%.

Top-band performance requires a robust live application, smooth interaction and edge-case handling, an engaging demonstration of key technical contributions, concise professional slides with diagrams and benchmark charts, confident delivery within the time limit, and technically authoritative Q&A answers.

## Mandatory policies stated in the rubric

- Academic dishonesty and plagiarism may result in mark deductions, subject failure, or disciplinary action.
- A formal GenAI Declaration Form must accompany every assessment deliverable listed as Proposal, Final Report, and Source Code.
- Students must document and cite tools used, prompt strategies, and the nature and extent of AI contributions versus original implementation.
- Late work loses 10% per 24-hour period and is not accepted after 72 hours, resulting in zero for that component.

Always re-check these policies against any later Project Specification or updated course notice.
